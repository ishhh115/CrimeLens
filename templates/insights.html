Technical Specifications

A review of model parameters, anomaly thresholds, and policy recommendations.

Machine Learning Architecture

The risk prediction engine uses a Random Forest Classifier trained on historical features engineered from annual crime records.

  • Class labels: Risk categories are defined by normalizing total crime volumes into four bands: Low (≤ 25), Medium (≤ 50), High (≤ 75), and Critical (> 75).
  • Engineered inputs: In addition to raw volumes, the classifier utilizes the YoY percentage changes and the ratio of violent crimes (Murder + Rape + Robbery) to total IPC cases.
  • Local Explainability: Features are extracted per prediction to show local impact percentages, helping municipal planners understand what drove a high/critical risk rating.

z-Score Anomaly Methodology

Statistical anomaly detection is performed at startup using calculated standard deviations from each state's historical mean volume:

z = (X - μ) / σ

Years where the absolute z-score exceeds 1.5 are flagged as anomalous. High positive z-scores represent unusual, non-linear crime spikes, indicating the potential influence of external factors (e.g., policy shifts, reporting changes, localized crises) that warrant targeted municipal investigation.

Future Roadmap

  • Transition from state-level aggregation to district-level drill-down capabilities for granular localized modeling.
  • Incorporate secondary external parameters (such as demographic density and local economic indices) to enrich feature weights.
  • Train recurrent deep-learning networks (LSTMs) for time-series volume forecasting to augment the classification output.
