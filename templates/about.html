About the CrimeLens System

A deeper look at the motivations, target problem, and codebase design decisions.

Why this project exists

Law enforcement and civic planning agencies face a perpetual challenge: municipal resource allocation is often reactive rather than preventative. CrimeLens was built to explore how combining standard historical statistical analysis (z-score testing) with predictive machine learning (Random Forest) can generate forward-looking insights to optimize patrol density and policy intervention strategies.

By compiling NCRB IPC records from 2001 to 2012, this case study uncovers structural trends, matches them against recent data points, and evaluates classification algorithms to support policy planning.

Core Capabilities

  • Regional Risk Classification: Categorizes state/UT safety risks from Low to Critical using trained decision trees.
  • YoY Anomaly Detection: Uses a 1.5 standard deviation z-score ceiling to identify sudden annual shifts in crime report rates.
  • Policy Recommendation Engine: Automatically profiles state-level dominant crime groups and offers targeted policing focus areas.
  • Spatial Visualization: Overlays calculated risk bands onto an interactive geographic shapefile mapping Indian state lines.
